01 — INTRODUCTION

The Elevator Pitch

Reigns meets Rockstar Ate My Hamster. Card-swipe band management. You've inherited money and decided to manage a British indie band. Every decision is a swipe — left or right. Build the band from pub back rooms to stadium stages across six in-game years. Early 2000s. Demo tapes. Sticky floors. No smartphones.

"A nostalgic celebration of a legendary era that doesn't get enough mention for how much has changed in music and social culture."

— Dave, playtester

"It definitely draws you in for the 'one more turn' feeling. The quick swipe decisions are very moreish."

— Early build playtester

Playtesters rated writing 4.5/5, engagement 4.3/5. Four of ten played to content exhaustion.

Format

Card-swipe decisions. Five meters (money, followers, morale, ego, reputation). Milestone-driven phases: sign a deal → Rising, enter the national chart → Peak. Four phases: Formation → Rising → Peak → Legacy.

Platform

Mobile-first (React Native/Expo). Android and iOS in active testing via OTA updates. Natural path to PC (Steam) and Switch. Reigns proved the format works across all platforms.

Status

v0.6.46. Formation and Rising fully playable. Peak partially implemented. Active playtesting, with one tester reaching week 261 (five in-game years) before hitting content limits. OTA updates shipping live to testers weekly.

Business Model

Premium purchase. No ads, no energy systems, no microtransactions. Optional paid content packs post-launch (new eras, licensed editions). The game respects the player's time and money.

03 — SYSTEMS

Beneath the Swipe

Gig Circuit

31 venues across 7 tiers — pub back rooms to stadiums. The band outgrows each tier to reach the next. Transparent receipts. Pre-show tension cards. Gigs give money and followers; meters only move through card decisions.

Recording & Release

Studio lockout with timed speed rounds. 96 recording cards. Procedural cover art. EP/album naming. Single selection. Two-chart sales engine with star-scaled decline curves. Quality earned through skill and gear.

Label Deals

10 labels across 5 tiers. EP → EP → album cycles. Recoupment, royalties, performance evaluation. Get dropped for poor sales. Choose to stay loyal or chase a bigger deal.

Publicity & Press

20 publicity stunt types. After every single, a promotional push — radio, record shops, TV appearances — with timed speed-round decisions. Bold choices boost sales; safe choices can hurt. 60+ newspaper headlines react to the band's moves.

Awards & Ceremonies

Brut Awards (February) and NMW Awards (September). Interactive ceremonies with speed-round decision moments during the show. Winning depends on chart performance. The moment the band realises they made it — or didn't.

Seasonal & Recurring

Christmas content live (61 cards, holiday break mechanics, festive chart battles). Superfan Susan recurring as the band's Number One Fan. More seasonal content in development: Valentine's, Easter, Bonfire Night, Jubilee 2002.

04 — IN-GAME

Every Card Is a Decision

The Writing

850+ unique cards across 8 pools, written to a strict style guide (Card Bible v5.2). Deadpan narrator who loves music. The humour comes from accuracy, not jokes. 60+ newspaper headlines. World-building cards for early 2000s texture.

The Era

January 2000. Demo tapes, fanzines, sticky floors. Napster, Friends Reunited, Nokia 3310s. Pre-smartphone, pre-streaming. The specificity is the point. A legendary era nobody has made a game about.

The Depth

Looks like a card game. Runs like a simulation. Rehearsal, gear, recording, sales, charts, labels, crises, band dynamics. 109 crisis cards when meters hit danger zones. 20+ multi-step narrative chains. The player doesn't see the systems — they just feel that decisions matter.

Target Audience

The Nostalgic

Adults 30–50 who were there. British indie alumni, music press readers, pub gig veterans, lapsed band members. They want to remember it, argue with it, feel it. The audience for whom this game is a memory.

The Category Fans

Premium indie mobile gamers and narrative game fans. The audience that bought Reigns, Florence, A Dark Room, Disco Elysium. They follow the format and want more of it, executed well. The audience for whom this game is a genre they trust.

The Culturally Curious

Younger players who didn't live through the era but are drawn to it through music, film, or parents' record collections. International audiences discovering British indie culture. The audience for whom this game is a window.

09 — WHAT COMES NEXT

Beyond the First Release

The engine and format are era-agnostic. The entire card-swipe band management system scales to other eras, genres, and cultures. If the first game works, the framework supports sequels and licensed editions.

Sequel Concepts

The format supports follow-up games. A modern-day version with contemporary artist parodies, social media, streaming royalties. The 1960s with rock ’n’ roll roots, psychedelia. The American indie underground of the early 90s. Same engine, new cards, new tone.

Licensed Editions

Real band stories in the same format. Precedent: Reigns × Game of Thrones, Reigns × The Witcher. The engine handles any music narrative — it needs the right IP partner. Documentary-style band histories, fictional music universes, or artist-approved career retrospectives.
